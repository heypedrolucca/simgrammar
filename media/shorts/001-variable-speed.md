# One mechanic in 60 seconds: Pause and speed control

Vertical, 60 seconds, about 150 words. Drawn from the record `data/mechanics/variable-speed.yaml`, which is in the calibration set. The animatic uses a scratch AI voice, disclosed on screen; the release uses the host's voice.

## 0:00 to 0:08. Hook

**Picture.** A colony under attack freezes mid-frame. A pause icon stamps the centre.

**Voice.** The most common mechanic in simulation games is a button that stops the game.{{assert:derived.variable-speed.prevalence_rank == 1}}

## 0:08 to 0:22. What it owns

**Picture.** A clock face with a multiplier dial: pause, 1x, 3x, 6x. Ticks pour through a funnel faster as the dial turns.

**Voice.** It owns one thing: how fast simulated time runs. Paused, nothing moves, but you can still give orders. Faster, the game runs more ticks every second.

## 0:22 to 0:36. The levers

**Picture.** Four levers slide up one at a time, each labelled.

**On screen.** How many speeds. Orders while paused. What pauses for you. Who controls speed online.

**Voice.** Designers tune four things: how many speeds, whether you can act while paused, which events pause the game for you, and who decides the speed in multiplayer.

## 0:36 to 0:48. How it fails

**Picture.** The speed dial says 6x; a meter beside it shows the simulation managing 3x and falling behind.

**Voice.** Its failure is honest: at top speed the machine can fall behind the number on the button, and you feel the cost of every other system in the game.
