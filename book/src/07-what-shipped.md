# Chapter 7. What shipped

You pick a fantasy setting and type that you draw your sword and step into the tower. A paragraph comes back describing the stairwell, the smell of smoke, and someone moving on the landing above. You type that you call out, and another paragraph follows. An hour later you mention the silver key you picked up near the start, and the story has no idea what you mean.

There was never a key, in any sense the program could check. [[ai-dungeon|AI Dungeon]] has no world state, no inventory, and no rules engine. Each turn it assembles a prompt from the scenario premise, whatever you pinned to its memory box, and the last stretch of the story, and a language model writes the next passage: GPT-2 with 1.5 billion parameters when AI Dungeon 2 launched in December 2019, GPT-3 for the premium model from July 2020 [@src-wikipedia-ai-dungeon]. A fact survives as long as it stays inside the model's context window. Your tools for keeping the story straight are undo, retry, edit, and the memory box, where you write down the key yourself.

Four years later [[suck-up|Suck Up!]] put a model behind a front door. You play a vampire who has to be invited in. You choose a disguise, knock, and talk into a real microphone; the resident answers out loud, in character, and decides whether to let you in. Each resident's personality is written by the developers. Each reply comes from a hosted language model given that personality and your words; the store page names ChatGPT, and the 2023 terms name GPT 3.5 and Llama 2 and meter use in tokens [@src-steam-2026-suck-up; @src-proxima-2023-suck-up-terms]. A disguise works because it changes what the model is told about you. Players write new residents and whole neighbourhoods and share them on Steam Workshop.

The dataset classes both games as boundary cases. In AI Dungeon nothing is simulated outside the model's text, and in Suck Up! the model decides whether your pitch worked, with no social system underneath keeping score. They show what a game looks like when the model is the whole game. The rest of the chapter covers games that have a simulation, and what AI has been allowed to do inside them.

## How the dataset counts maturity

A technique enters the dataset only as a dated, citable result that changes at least one mechanic. There are {{count:technique}} of them, from tree search and wave-function collapse to world models that generate a playable scene from a sentence. Each carries a maturity judged for deployment in games, which is a stricter standard than maturity in AI generally. Constrained decoding, which forces a model's output into a fixed format, is routine in software engineering and rare in shipped game runtimes, so it is scored as research here.{{assert:field.constrained-decoding.maturity == research}}

The scale has four steps. *Research* means papers and lab demos. *Prototype* means public playable demos or tech previews with no sustained commercial title. *Shipped (niche)* means at least one commercial game uses it at runtime or in production. *Shipped (mainstream)* means it is common across studios.

## The one norm: frames

The one learned technique that has become a genre norm is neural rendering. Upscaling reconstructs a sharp frame from a lower-resolution render and the motion of each pixel; frame generation predicts whole frames between the ones the game rendered; small networks inside shaders decode compressed textures and denoise ray-traced light. By January 2026 more than 250 titles supported multi-frame generation and more than 400 games and apps supported upscaling [@src-nvidia-2026-dlss-4-5]. Simulation games are among them: Microsoft Flight Simulator (2020) supports both upscaling and frame generation, and Cities: Skylines II supports upscaling [@src-pcgamingwiki-2026-msfs-2020; @src-pcgamingwiki-2026-cities-skylines-2].{{assert:field.neural-rendering-shaders.maturity == shipped-mainstream}}

A generated frame is drawn between two frames the game computed, and the simulation never learns it exists. Nothing about the rules, the saves, or the multiplayer code has to change, which is why a studio can add it late in development and why the dataset records it as preserving determinism.{{assert:field.neural-rendering-shaders.determinism_impact == preserves}} The same separation shows in the one place it has gone badly. DLSS 5, which launched in September 2026 with a final network pass that adds lighting and material detail to the finished frame, drew a largely negative reaction from players and developers over altered faces and art direction [@src-nvidia-2026-dlss-5; @src-wikipedia-deep-learning-super-sampling]. The complaint was about how the game looked, and the simulation was untouched either way.

## Assets, places, and motion

The next group changes what a simulation is drawn with, and most of it runs before the player starts.

Microsoft Flight Simulator (2020) rebuilt the planet from map data. Machine learning running on Azure classified buildings, trees, and terrain in Bing Maps imagery, and procedural rules turned them into about 1.5 billion buildings and 2 trillion trees, streamed to the player from more than two petabytes of data as they fly [@src-wikipedia-microsoft-flight-simulator-2020]. The reconstruction is done offline and the result is fixed data, so two players flying over the same town see the same houses.{{assert:field.satellite-world-reconstruction.maturity == shipped-niche}}{{assert:field.satellite-world-reconstruction.determinism_impact == preserves}}

Text-to-3D models such as TRELLIS turn a prompt or an image into a textured mesh in seconds to a minute on a large GPU [@src-xiang-2024-trellis]. [[inzoi|inZOI]] exposes the idea to players: a 2D image becomes a 3D object in the game, and an uploaded video becomes a character's motion [@src-steam-2025-inzoi]. A generated asset has to be stored to be reproduced, since asking the model again gives a different chair, so the dataset marks the technique as mitigable rather than safe.{{assert:field.text-to-3d-assets.determinism_impact == mitigable}}

Learned motion has shipped in sports and life games. Ubisoft's learned motion matching replaced a 590 MB database of motion-capture frames with about 17 MB of network weights and nearly identical animation [@src-ubisoft-2020-learned-motion-matching]. FIFA 22's HyperMotion generated player animation in real time from a model trained on more than 8.7 million frames of full-team match capture [@src-wikipedia-fifa-22]. No physics is simulated in either case; the networks choose the next pose.

This is also where most of the store's AI activity sits. About a thousand Steam games had disclosed generative AI by April 2024 [@src-totallyhuman-2024-steam-genai-count]. By July 2025 the count was 7,818 titles, about 7 percent of the store, with just under a fifth of that year's releases disclosing and visual assets named in about 60 percent of disclosures [@src-totallyhuman-2025-steam-genai-count]. Valve's content survey separates pre-generated content from content generated live during play and asks developers of the second kind to describe their guardrails [@src-valve-2026-steamworks-content-survey].

## Characters that talk

Runtime language characters did ship, in a handful of titles over two years.

In inZOI, released into early access in March 2025, a small language model running on the player's graphics card lets typed prompts steer a Zoi's thoughts and actions [@src-steam-2025-inzoi]. NVIDIA describes these Smart Zois as agents that plan, act, and reflect on their decisions [@src-nvidia-2026-ace-developer-page]. The model chooses among the game's own actions: the needs, the animations, and the action set are inZOI's, and the model replaces the hand-written scoring that picks the next one.{{assert:field.on-device-language-models.maturity == shipped-niche}} [[where-winds-meet|Where Winds Meet]], a martial-arts RPG, discloses that its NPCs answer free text and voice through a live model [@src-steam-2025-where-winds-meet], while its quests and combat stay authored and rule-based. [[whispers-from-the-star|Whispers from the Star]] is built around one character, the stranded astronaut Stella, whose spoken replies and choices are generated in a voice developed with an actor, behind safety filters; whether she survives depends on what you tell her [@src-steam-2026-whispers-from-the-star]. Outside the catalog, KRAFTON ran a two-week public beta in June 2026 of PUBG Ally, a teammate that takes spoken orders through an on-device model [@src-krafton-2026-pubg-ally-beta].

Two costs fell in that window: the price of voice and the size of a usable model. One leading realtime speech API cut its audio prices from 100 and 200 dollars per million input and output tokens at launch in October 2024 to 32 and 64 dollars in August 2025 [@src-openai-2024-realtime-api; @src-openai-2025-gpt-realtime]. On the device side, pruning and distillation produced instruction models small enough that a 2-billion-parameter one fits in about 1.5 GB of video memory beside the game's own rendering [@src-sreenivas-2024-minitron; @src-nvidia-2025-ace-companions], although NVIDIA's in-game inference kit still asks for at least an 8 GB card from the RTX 30 series [@src-nvidia-2026-nvigi-core].

The determinism record splits this group. A local model can be seeded and run the same way twice, so on-device characters are mitigable. Characters built as full generative agents, language-model game masters, and realtime voice are recorded as breaking reproducibility outright: the same input can produce a different reply, and a replay has to store what was said.{{assert:field.on-device-language-models.determinism_impact == mitigable}}{{assert:field.generative-agents.determinism_impact == breaks}}{{assert:field.llm-game-master.determinism_impact == breaks}}{{assert:field.realtime-voice-models.determinism_impact == breaks}}

A grounded variant of the language-model storyteller was in early access by August 2025. Hidden Door runs each player intent through planning, a check against the setting's canon and rules, and an update to a typed world state before any text is shown; its example blocks Dracula from entering a house he was not invited into. The company reports that a 1.7-billion-parameter model tuned on real play beat a 120-billion-parameter general model on one of its core narrative tasks [@src-hiddendoor-2026-blog-index; @src-hiddendoor-2026-homepage]. Hidden Door is outside the catalog, but its order of operations, state first and prose second, is the one later chapters recommend.

## Societies in the lab

Two catalog entries are research demonstrations rather than products, and they show what language-model characters do when there are many of them. In [[generative-agents-smallville|Generative Agents]], a Stanford and Google team put 25 language-model characters in a Sims-like town. Each stores what it sees as text, retrieves memories by how recent, important, and relevant they are, writes reflections when enough important things have happened, and plans its day in hour and then five-to-fifteen-minute chunks. Seeding one agent with the plan to host a Valentine's Day party led 13 of the 25 to hear about it and five to turn up, over two game days and at a cost of thousands of dollars in API credits [@src-park-2023-generative-agents]. [[project-sid|Project Sid]] ran up to a thousand agents in Minecraft. In a 29-agent run with a constitution and a 20 percent tax, influencers argued for and against the tax, amendments passed, and payments followed the new rate; in a 500-agent run, a religion seeded by 20 priests spread to other towns [@src-altera-2024-project-sid].

Both are research runs: they need a server, they cost real money per hour, and neither can be replayed exactly. The dataset records the generative-agent technique as shipped on the strength of inZOI, whose Smart Zois plan, act, and reflect with a small local model.

## Opponents and tools

The oldest shipped techniques in the set are search and learning methods aimed at opponents. Monte Carlo tree search, introduced in 2006, plans by playing many random games forward from the current position and backing up which moves won [@src-kocsis-2006-uct]. Total War: Rome II used it for campaign decisions and Fable Legends for tactical planning [@src-wikipedia-monte-carlo-tree-search].

The best-known learned opponent in the catalog is Gran Turismo Sophy. Sony AI trained it with deep reinforcement learning on many parallel copies of the Gran Turismo simulator, with a reward that values course progress and encodes racing's sportsmanship rules, and in 2022 it beat four of the world's best Gran Turismo drivers head to head [@src-wurman-2022-gt-sophy]. It reached players in [[gran-turismo-7|Gran Turismo 7]] as a time-limited event in February 2023 [@src-polyphony-2023-gt-sophy-race-together] and became a permanent Quick Race rival that November on nine tracks and 340 cars [@src-polyphony-2023-gt-sophy-2], then moved into custom races in 2025. Sophy drives the same tire, suspension, and weight-transfer model the player does, so the dataset records it as deepening vehicle dynamics and embodied skill rather than replacing either.{{assert:field.deep-rl-npc-policies.maturity == shipped-niche}}{{assert:field.deep-rl-npc-policies.determinism_impact == mitigable}}

Raw strength is rarely what a game needs from an opponent, which is why so few of these methods have shipped despite their results. A studio asks whether the opponent is fair, whether it can be tuned to a player's level, and whether it fits inside a content update, and Sophy arrived one update at a time with Beginner, Intermediate, and Expert settings [@src-polyphony-2023-gt-sophy-race-together].

Two tools shipped on the developer's side. Agent-based playtesting predicts difficulty and finds exploits before release: a network trained on logged moves in Candy Crush Saga predicted level difficulty better than tree search at a fraction of the compute [@src-gudmundsson-2018-human-like-playtesting], and EA used search agents to balance careers in The Sims Mobile [@src-zhao-2019-winning-isnt-everything]. Wave-function collapse, a constraint solver that fills a grid tile by tile from an example, generates levels in Bad North and Caves of Qud and runs live in [[townscaper|Townscaper]], where every block you click reruns the solver over the cells around it [@src-gumin-2016-wavefunctioncollapse; @src-thompson-2022-how-townscaper-works]. Both are deterministic given their seeds.{{assert:field.automated-playtesting.determinism_impact == preserves}}{{assert:field.wave-function-collapse.determinism_impact == preserves}}

## What writes into the simulation

Sort the shipped techniques by whether they change authoritative game state, the state that saves, replays, and multiplayer depend on, and the list is short. A cached model output can become a rule, as in [[infinite-craft|Infinite Craft]], where the first answer to a new combination is stored and served to every later player; chapter 8 opens there.{{assert:field.llm-rule-adjudication.maturity == shipped-niche}} Language-model characters choose actions in inZOI and a few AI-native titles. Search and learned policies drive opponents. Everything else in the shipped group changes what the player sees and hears, or does its work before release.

The {{stat:n_technique_shipped}} shipped techniques affect {{stat:n_mechanics_touched_by_shipped}} mechanics, and those mechanics account for {{stat:pct_uses_touched_by_shipped}} percent of the uses in the catalog.{{assert:pct_uses_touched_by_shipped < 50}} Of the {{stat:n_effect_replaces_substrate}} edges in which a technique would replace how a mechanic works, {{stat:n_replaces_substrate_shipped}} come from techniques that have shipped.

No learned replacement for physics, fields, or the world itself runs in a catalog game. The models that would replace the engine outright are prototypes. GameNGen simulated DOOM at 20 frames per second on a single TPU, and human raters picked the real game over the simulation only 58 to 60 percent of the time on short clips; its authors state it cannot be used to create new games [@src-valevski-2024-gamengen]. Genie 3 renders 720p at 24 frames per second from a text prompt and keeps about a minute of visual memory [@src-deepmind-2025-genie-3]. When Google opened it to subscribers as Project Genie in January 2026, each world allowed sixty seconds of play, a limit DeepMind attributed to compute, and a reviewer watched characters walk through walls [@src-techcrunch-2026-project-genie]. Microsoft's WHAMM ran a section of one Quake II level at more than ten frames per second with blurry enemies, damage that could come out wrong, and health counters that could not be trusted [@src-microsoft-2025-whamm].{{assert:field.genie-world-models.maturity == prototype}}{{assert:field.diffusion-game-engines.maturity == prototype}}{{assert:field.muse-wham.maturity == prototype}} Learned physics is further back: subspace neural physics has a prototype, and graph-network simulators remain research.{{assert:field.subspace-neural-physics.maturity == prototype}}{{assert:field.graph-network-simulators.maturity == research}}

A world model holds the game's state as pixels. Position, health, and the layout of the map exist only in the frames it remembers, so anything that has scrolled out of that memory can come back different. Simulation games depend on the opposite property: a cow in a field on day one is the same cow on day three hundred, with a history the player can inspect.

## Reproducibility

Of the {{stat:n_technique_runtime}} techniques in the set that can run during play, {{stat:n_technique_runtime_breaks}} break reproducibility outright, {{stat:n_technique_runtime_mitigable}} can be made reproducible by seeding, caching, or logging their outputs, and {{stat:n_technique_runtime_preserves}} leave it intact.{{assert:n_technique_runtime_breaks < n_technique_runtime_mitigable}} Among the shipped ones, {{stat:n_technique_shipped_breaks}} break it: generative agents, language-model game masters, and realtime voice.{{assert:n_technique_shipped_breaks == 3}}

The cache in Infinite Craft shows how a mitigable technique is made safe. The model is consulted once per new pair and its answer is written to a database, so the game behaves like a growing rule table that every player shares. The same move works elsewhere: record each model output as an event, and a replay reads the record instead of asking the model again. Chapter 11 returns to determinism as one of the places where AI in simulation breaks.

## How it was received

Developers' opinion moved against generative AI while their use of it held steady. In GDC's surveys, 36 percent of respondents reported using it in both 2025 and 2026, and the share who believe it harms the industry rose from 30 to 52 percent [@src-gdc-2025-state-of-industry; @src-gdc-2026-state-of-industry]. inZOI sold strongly at launch and drew negative user reviews after Krafton announced its generative features [@src-wikipedia-inzoi]. ARC Raiders was criticized for synthetic voice lines, some of which were later re-recorded by actors [@src-wikipedia-arc-raiders], and the SAG-AFTRA video game strike that ran from July 2024 to July 2025 ended with contract terms on consent for AI voice replicas [@src-wikipedia-artificial-intelligence-in-video-games]. AI Dungeon had met the moderation side of the problem earlier. Its 2021 content filter, added to block sexual content involving minors, flagged innocent text, and human moderators read the flagged private stories, which led to a wave of negative reviews [@src-wikipedia-ai-dungeon]. Games with disclosures still sold: an estimate in November 2025 put them at roughly 8 percent of the store with about 660 million dollars in combined gross [@src-totallyhuman-2025-steam-ai-revenue].

## Exercise: classify an AI feature

Pick a game with an AI feature, from the catalog or from a recent announcement.

1. Name the mechanic it changes, using the catalog's names. If it changes none, it belongs to presentation.
2. Decide whether it writes authoritative state (something a save file or a replay must contain) or only changes what the player sees and hears.
3. Note when it runs: before release, while the player builds, or during play.
4. Ask what happens to a replay, a multiplayer session, or a bug report if the model answers differently tomorrow. If the answer is "it breaks", find the log or cache that would fix it.

The features that pass step 2 are rare, and almost all of them sit in agents, narrative, and opponents. Chapter 8 counts every technique in the set, shipped or not, against every mechanic, and finds that the families most games are built from are the ones the field has worked on least.
