# Chapter 9. Vacant lots

You lay a road along the river in [[cities-skylines|Cities: Skylines]] and paint residential zones beside it. Houses go up, families move in, and within a few in-game years the district is full. You add a school, a clinic, and a bus line. Each citizen picks the fastest route to work, and the jams appear exactly where your road plan was weakest.

Decades later something odd happens. Hearses start backing up across the district, and houses with a body inside show a warning icon. The people who moved in together have grown old together, and the cohort is dying at once; the record calls it "an initial cohort moving in together" that "can age into a wave of deaths that overloads hearses." The game presents this as a service problem: build more deathcare. Nothing in it registers as a story about the people involved. None of the game's {{derived:cities-skylines.mechanic_count}} mechanics fires an event because of what you built or where you built it, and there is no mechanism for one.

That absence is common. Building placement appears in {{derived:free-placement.prevalence}} catalog games, and triggered event chains, the mechanism that lets Crusader Kings III follow a rival's illness with a succession dispute and then a war, appear in {{derived:triggered-event-chains.prevalence}}. If the two were spread across games independently, several games would have both. One does: [[surviving-mars|Surviving Mars]], whose Mysteries lay authored storylines such as plagues, rival corporations, AI revolts, and alien contact over a colony the player lays out.{{assert:cooccur.free-placement.triggered-event-chains == 1}}{{assert:rank.whitespace.free-placement.triggered-event-chains == 1}} Even there, the Mysteries are layered on top of the colony, and the record does not tie any of them to where a building stands.

Call these vacant lots: pairs of mechanics that are each common, belong to different parts of the design, and almost never share a game.

## Packages

Start with the opposite case, pairs that turn up together far more often than chance predicts. The dataset measures this as lift: how often two mechanics share a game, divided by how often they would if each were spread across the catalog independently. Pairs that share fewer than three games are left out, so that a single unusual game cannot make a pair look strong.

{{table:cooccurrence-top}}

The strongest pairs are packages inherited from one lineage. Sports management carries a trio: an engine that resolves matches from player attributes, a board whose confidence in the manager rises and falls with results, and contract negotiation.{{assert:rank.cooccurrence-top.attribute-match-engine.board-confidence <= 10}} Grand strategy carries war score, territorial control, and religious and cultural conversion.{{assert:rank.cooccurrence-top.culture-religion-conversion.war-score-peace <= 10}} The SimCity core of land value, demand, and traffic travels as a unit through the city builders.{{assert:rank.cooccurrence-top.land-value.traffic-congestion <= 15}} The Sims pairs aspirations with smart objects, the objects that advertise what they offer to the characters nearby,{{assert:rank.cooccurrence-top.aspiration-wants.smart-objects <= 10}} and virtual pets pair care with learning.{{assert:rank.cooccurrence-top.creature-care.creature-learning <= 15}}

Further down the table the same pattern holds in smaller lineages. Flight sims pair aerodynamics with procedural checklists, the cockpit routines a pilot runs before each phase of flight.{{assert:rank.cooccurrence-top.flight-aerodynamics.procedural-checklists <= 30}} Persistent online worlds pair shared state with player organizations, the corporations and guilds of EVE Online and Ultima Online.{{assert:rank.cooccurrence-top.persistent-world.player-organizations <= 30}} Colony sims pair designation orders with rooms the game recognizes from their walls and furniture.{{assert:rank.cooccurrence-top.designation-orders.room-recognition <= 30}} Hospital games pair diagnosis with queues of waiting patients.{{assert:rank.cooccurrence-top.diagnosis-treatment.service-queues <= 30}} The AI-native titles pair generated content with gossip that spreads from character to character.{{assert:rank.cooccurrence-top.generative-content.gossip-propagation <= 30}}

The mechanics in a package solve one problem together. In SimCity, land value is computed per block from distance to the centre, nearby trees and water, pollution, and crime, and traffic writes pollution into the same map; a road plan therefore changes what the land is worth, and the player reads both from overlays. Take the land value away and traffic becomes a nuisance with no economic consequence. Take traffic away and land value loses one of its sources of change. Designers in the lineage keep both because each makes the other matter.

Packages also show how slowly the vocabulary recombines. A designer who starts a city builder inherits zoning, demand, land value, and traffic; one who starts a grand strategy game inherits war score and conversion. Packages are part of why later games reuse old parts (chapter 3) and why games cluster into families (chapter 4): a family is held together by mechanics that rarely travel alone.

## Lots no one has built

Most possible pairs of mechanics have never shared a game, and most of those empty pairs are uninteresting, because at least one of the two mechanics is rare. The white-space analysis keeps only the pairs where the emptiness means something. Both mechanics must appear in at least {{stat:whitespace_min_games}} catalog games. They must come from different families, since two mechanics from the same family are often alternatives. Neither may be a time-and-pacing control, because a game's time model already decides them. The pair must not be recorded as being in design tension: the dataset holds {{count:edge:tension}} such edges, such as building assistance that favours organic shapes against strict structural rules that punish them. And the two must share at most one game. {{stat:n_whitespace_pairs}} pairs pass.{{assert:n_whitespace_pairs >= 100}}

The tension edges record what designers already know about pairs that fight. Each carries a note. Threat that scales with wealth pulls against milestone unlocks: "One rewards growth, the other punishes it; tuning both sets the game's attitude to expansion." Agents with strong free will resist direct commands, so a game with both has to decide who is in charge. Full-information overlays undercut fog of war. A shared persistent world cannot be paused or sped up by one player. A storyteller's directed randomness makes scores incomparable across players, which sits badly with the global histograms of programming puzzles. Generated content resists the authored preconditions that event chains rely on. A pair in tension can still be built, but the white-space table leaves these out because their emptiness has an explanation on record.

Built lots are pairs that share games, darker where they share more than chance predicts. Dashed lots are white space, slashed lots are pairs recorded as being in tension, and numbers mark the ten largest gaps.

Expected co-occurrence treats each mechanic as spread across games independently of how many mechanics each game has. Games with many mechanics make every pair more likely, so a pair whose mechanics sit mostly in small games can look emptier than it is. A null model that preserved each game's size would shorten the list, and the dataset has not built one yet. The threshold is a choice too: {{stat:whitespace_min_games}} games was 8 percent of the catalog when it was fixed, and the analysis file records that it should be revisited as the catalog grows. A lot can also look empty because of where the vocabulary draws its lines; the Factorio blueprints in the next section are an example. Read the table as a list of candidates, ranked by a simple measure.

## Five lots

**Placement and events.** The top lot is the one this chapter opened with. City builders give the player detailed control over where things go and almost never tell a story about it; event-driven games tell stories and rarely give the player a map to build on. A tavern next to the temple, or a mill whose pond floods a farmer's field, could fire an event about the specific people it affects, with choices whose effects are typed and bounded: a change in happiness, in a faction's standing, in a resource. A chronicle would record each one, so a district would build up a history the player could read back. The dataset records that idea as an opportunity, with Frostpunk, Manor Lords, Islanders, and Crusader Kings III as partial precedents, and chapter 10 ranks it.

**Skill and placement.** The second lot pairs embodied skill control, the hand-flying and driving of flight and racing sims, with building placement.{{assert:rank.whitespace.embodied-skill-control.free-placement <= 3}}{{assert:cooccur.embodied-skill-control.free-placement == 0}} This lot may well be empty for a reason. The games with embodied skill are flight sims, driving sims, and a handful of physics sandboxes, and most of them have no use for placing buildings. The neighbouring lot is built: [[kerbal-space-program|Kerbal Space Program]] and [[besiege|Besiege]] both have the player assemble a machine from parts and then fly or drive it by hand, which the dataset records as modular assembly next to embodied skill.{{assert:cooccur.modular-assembly.embodied-skill-control >= 3}} What nobody in the catalog has built is the version where the hands-on part happens inside a place the player laid out, a racetrack or an airfield or a town, rather than inside a vehicle.

**Workers who age, in factories.** Lifecycle and aging, in which agents are born, grow through life stages, and die, appears in {{derived:lifecycle-aging.prevalence}} games, and production chains in {{derived:production-chain.prevalence}}. No catalog game has both.{{assert:cooccur.lifecycle-aging.production-chain == 0}}{{assert:rank.whitespace.lifecycle-aging.production-chain <= 5}} Factory games are run by machines or faceless workers. Games with aging populations run economies without modelled production chains. [[banished|Banished]] comes closest from that side: every worker is born, needs a house to start a family, ages, and dies, so the town's age structure produces labour booms and shortages years after the decision that caused them. Its families gather, farm, and stockpile food, firewood, clothing, and tools, but nothing in it is recorded as a multi-step production chain.{{assert:cooccur.lifecycle-aging.production-chain == 0}} The closest cases are industrial colony builders. In [[captain-of-industry|Captain of Industry]], residents need housing, food, power, healthcare, and household goods, and their satisfaction produces the currency that buys edicts and expansion. In [[workers-and-resources|Workers & Resources: Soviet Republic]], more than thirty commodities move by truck, rail, conveyor, and pipe through staffed plants. Neither record lists workers who age and retire. A factory whose throughput depends on a workforce with skills, households, and turnover would make a bottleneck as likely to be a missing mentor as a missing machine.

**Shared designs, in production games.** Content sharing, where player creations are uploaded, browsed, rated, and imported into other players' games, appears in {{derived:content-sharing.prevalence}} games, mostly sandboxes, life sims, contraption builders, and management games.{{assert:cooccur.content-sharing.production-chain == 1}}{{assert:rank.whitespace.content-sharing.production-chain <= 10}} The one production game with it is [[spacechem|SpaceChem]], whose solutions are plotted on global histograms of other players' results and whose ResearchNet let players share puzzles. [[factorio|Factorio]] players do exchange layouts: selected areas save to blueprints, books, and a personal library, and exchange strings are shared online. The dataset records that as blueprints. What no production game in the catalog has is the browsable, rated catalog that Spore built for creatures or that Steam Workshop provides for Cities: Skylines, a place where a factory layout is published, remixed, and ranked.

**Research by people.** Tech trees appear in {{derived:tech-tree.prevalence}} games and relationship values in {{derived:relationship-values.prevalence}}; they share one, [[crusader-kings-3|Crusader Kings III]].{{assert:cooccur.relationship-values.tech-tree == 1}}{{assert:rank.whitespace.relationship-values.tech-tree <= 10}} In most games research is a currency spent on a graph. In colony and life sims, relationships between named characters drive much of what happens. Two catalog games get partway there. In [[stellaris|Stellaris]], each research field offers three semi-random project cards weighted in part by the traits of the scientist leading it. In [[game-dev-tycoon|Game Dev Tycoon]], the designers and programmers the player hires carry design, technology, and speed stats and are assigned to development work, while research points come from a separate pool. A research system run by a few named scientists with skills, collaborations, and grudges would turn progression into a social system, and its breakthroughs into events with names attached.

Permanent death and scenario objectives never share a game:{{assert:cooccur.permadeath.scenario-objectives == 0}} the games with permadeath are mostly colony sims, survival games, life sims, and virtual pets, and the games with scenario objectives are mostly city builders, tycoons, and vehicle sims. A treasury and growing crops never share one either:{{assert:cooccur.budget-treasury.crop-cultivation == 0}} here city builders are the largest group among the crop games and among the treasury games, and none has both. The first empty pair separates two lineages; the second runs through the middle of one genre.

## Lots already built

Some of the catalog's games already stand on lots that no other catalog game has built. [[prison-architect|Prison Architect]] is the only one that both hires staff one by one and wires a power grid, and the only one that pairs a treasury with a shared catalog of player creations: players share their prisons online, and Escape Mode lets other players try to break out of them.{{assert:cooccur.staff-hiring.power-grid == 1}}{{assert:cooccur.budget-treasury.content-sharing == 1}} [[kenshi|Kenshi]], an open-world RPG with no hero character, in which any recruit can starve, lose limbs, or be enslaved, is the only one where production chains meet permanent death.{{assert:cooccur.permadeath.production-chain == 1}} [[against-the-storm|Against the Storm]] is the only one that sets scenario objectives on procedurally generated terrain: each settlement is won by filling a reputation gauge before the Queen's patience runs out, on a map generated for that settlement.{{assert:cooccur.procedural-terrain.scenario-objectives == 1}}

Each of those pairs qualifies as white space, because the definition allows a single shared game. The table therefore holds two kinds of lot: the ones nobody has built and the ones exactly one game has.

## What makes a lot worth building

The catalog is a sample. It favours games that are well documented in English, and a game outside it may already combine any of these pairs. That is the cheapest way to close a lot: find the game, add it to the catalog with its sources, and the build recomputes the table. If the pair stops qualifying, every sentence in this chapter that depends on it fails its assertion and has to be rewritten.

A lot is worth building when the two mechanics feed each other. In the SimCity package, traffic lowers land value, and land value decides where the city grows and so where the traffic goes. Placement and events would feed each other if the events changed what the player builds next. Aging and production would if the workforce's age structure set the factory's throughput years later, the same demographic echo that fills Cities: Skylines' hearses, with a production consequence.

Several of the largest gaps involve production chains, treasuries, and crops,{{assert:rank.whitespace.lifecycle-aging.production-chain <= 5}}{{assert:rank.whitespace.content-sharing.production-chain <= 10}}{{assert:rank.whitespace.budget-treasury.crop-cultivation <= 10}} the economic mechanics that chapter 8 found AI techniques have touched least.{{assert:derived.production-chain.technique_exposure == 0}}{{assert:derived.budget-treasury.technique_exposure == 0}}{{assert:derived.crop-cultivation.technique_exposure == 0}} The two observations come from different data, the co-occurrence of mechanics in games and the edges from techniques to mechanics, and they point at the same part of the vocabulary.
