### 7.2 Adoption and reception

Generative AI has moved from novelty to a measurable share of releases. Disclosures on Steam rose from about a thousand games in April 2024 [@src-totallyhuman-2024-steam-genai-count] to 7,818 titles by July 2025, about 7 percent of the store, with just under a fifth of 2025 releases disclosing [@src-totallyhuman-2025-steam-genai-count]; and an estimate in November 2025 put games with disclosures at roughly 8 percent of the store with about 660 million dollars in combined gross [@src-totallyhuman-2025-steam-ai-revenue]. Most disclosures concern pre-generated visual assets rather than runtime systems. Valve's survey distinguishes pre-generated from live-generated content and requires the latter to describe its guardrails [@src-valve-2026-steamworks-content-survey].

Developer sentiment moved the other way. In GDC's surveys, 36 percent of respondents reported using generative AI themselves in both 2025 and 2026, while the share who believe it harms the industry rose to 52 percent from 30 percent [@src-gdc-2025-state-of-industry; @src-gdc-2026-state-of-industry]. Player reception has been mixed in visible cases: [[inzoi]] sold strongly at launch and drew negative reviews after announcing generative features [@src-wikipedia-inzoi]; ARC Raiders was criticized for synthetic voice lines, some of which were later re-recorded by actors [@src-wikipedia-arc-raiders].

Runtime language characters did ship. On-device small language models power simulated people in inZOI's Smart Zoi and companions in NARAKA's teammate and the PUBG Ally limited beta of June 2026 [@src-nvidia-2025-ace-companions; @src-krafton-2026-pubg-ally-beta]; [[where-winds-meet]] discloses live NPC text and voice chat [@src-steam-2025-where-winds-meet]. Audio prices for one leading realtime speech API fell from 100 and 200 dollars per million input and output tokens at launch in October 2024 to 32 and 64 dollars in August 2025, about 68 percent [@src-openai-2024-realtime-api; @src-openai-2025-gpt-realtime], and specialized text-to-speech vendors price in cents per minute [@src-inworld-2026-tts-1-5]. Cost limits frame-generating world models: the consumer Genie product grants sixty seconds of play per world, which DeepMind attributed to reaching more users on limited compute [@src-techcrunch-2026-project-genie].

Among rendering techniques, neural upscaling and frame generation are the one area where learned models are a norm, with hundreds of supported titles [@src-nvidia-2026-dlss-4-5]. They never touch simulation state, which is why they could spread.

### 7.3 Seven clusters

**Frame-generating world models.** [[diffusion-game-engines]], [[genie-world-models]], [[muse-wham]], and [[open-interactive-video-models]] learn to predict the next frame from recent frames and player input, so the model is the engine. They produce convincing short interactions and fail at what simulation games need most: exact persistent state, long memory, and inspectable causes. Their realistic near-term role is ideation, previsualization, and non-authoritative views (§9).

**3D, rendering, and assets.** [[gaussian-splatting]], [[generated-3d-worlds]], [[text-to-3d-assets]], [[neural-rendering-shaders]], [[real-time-video-restyling]], and [[satellite-world-reconstruction]] cut the authoring cost of what the simulation is drawn with, leaving rules untouched. They are the most mature group. Most preserve determinism; [[text-to-3d-assets]] and [[real-time-video-restyling]] are mitigable, since a generated asset or frame must be stored to be reproduced. Their main risks are provenance and reception.{{assert:field.text-to-3d-assets.determinism_impact == mitigable}}{{assert:field.real-time-video-restyling.determinism_impact == mitigable}}

**Motion.** [[learned-motion-synthesis]] has shipped in sports and life games; [[physics-based-character-control]] remains research. Both matter to simulation games because canned animation is how most agents' internal states are (poorly) displayed.

**Language agents and dialogue.** [[generative-agents]], [[llm-agent-societies]], [[human-behavior-simulation-models]], [[on-device-language-models]], [[constrained-decoding]], [[realtime-voice-models]], [[llm-game-master]], [[llm-explanation-of-state]], and [[llm-rule-adjudication]] change who decides what agents do and say, who narrates, and who explains. They relax the authoring and believability ceilings at a cost in consistency. Their determinism cost varies: [[generative-agents]], [[llm-agent-societies]], [[llm-game-master]], and [[realtime-voice-models]] break reproducibility; [[on-device-language-models]] and [[llm-rule-adjudication]] are mitigable because a local model can be seeded and a cached rule is replayed rather than regenerated.{{assert:field.generative-agents.determinism_impact == breaks}}{{assert:field.llm-game-master.determinism_impact == breaks}}{{assert:field.on-device-language-models.determinism_impact == mitigable}}{{assert:field.llm-rule-adjudication.determinism_impact == mitigable}}

**Planning and game theory.** [[monte-carlo-tree-search]], [[model-based-planning-muzero]], [[imperfect-information-solvers]], [[cicero-strategic-dialogue]], [[deep-rl-npc-policies]], and [[mean-field-games]] produce strong opponents and equilibrium-consistent populations. Their game deployments are few because strength is rarely the design goal; believable and tunable opponents are.

**Design automation.** [[wave-function-collapse]], [[quality-diversity-search]], [[learned-level-generation]], [[automated-rule-generation]], [[automated-playtesting]], [[open-ended-environment-generation]], [[voyager-skill-libraries]], [[generalist-game-agents]], and [[alphaevolve-algorithm-discovery]] mostly work on the developer's side: generating content and rules, finding exploits, and estimating difficulty. Used offline they preserve determinism. Some also run during play ([[wave-function-collapse]] in [[townscaper]], agents that play alongside the player). Deterministic algorithms such as wave-function collapse stay reproducible there; learned generators and agents need seeds or logs.

**Simulation science.** [[massively-parallel-simulation]], [[differentiable-simulation]], [[graph-network-simulators]], [[subspace-neural-physics]], [[ml-weather-models]], [[neural-cellular-automata]], [[continuous-alife-and-asal]], and [[simulation-based-inference]] come from robotics, physics, climate, and artificial life. They relax the compute ceiling and make calibration possible, and they are the least connected to game development today: an operational ML weather model runs at about a thousandth of the energy of the physics model it complements [@src-ecmwf-2025-aifs-operational], yet none of these techniques is confirmed at runtime in a catalog game.
