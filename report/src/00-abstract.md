## Abstract

Simulation games are built from a shared but undocumented vocabulary of rule-systems. This report makes that vocabulary explicit and machine-readable, then asks how mathematical, AI, ML, and LLM results from 2019 to 2026 change it. We decompose {{count:game}} simulation games ({{stat:year_min}} to {{stat:year_max}}, {{stat:n_subgenres}} subgenres) into {{count:mechanic}} atomic mechanics, analyze each mechanic on its own (definition, levers, failure modes, dynamics, mathematical basis, and six anchored scores), map {{count:technique}} techniques onto mechanics through {{stat:n_affects_edges}} typed effect edges, and synthesize {{count:opportunity}} design opportunities, each with a validation experiment and a dated, falsifiable prediction. All results are projections of a versioned dataset with provenance for every entity and a validator that keeps the report, wiki, tables, and graph consistent.

**Findings.**

1. **Simulation games are compositions of six layers**: substrate, dynamics, coupling, control, legibility, and pacing. Clustering games by the mechanics they use recovers design families (minds, builders, operators, planners) that cut across market subgenres; {{stat:n_clusters_cross_subgenre}} of {{stat:n_clusters}} clusters mix subgenres.{{assert:n_clusters_cross_subgenre >= 7}}
2. **The vocabulary was set early.** {{stat:pct_mechanics_seen_by_2000}} percent of mechanics appear in a catalog game released by 2000.{{assert:pct_mechanics_seen_by_2000 >= 60}} Later decades mostly deepened and recombined; {{stat:pair_coverage_pct}} percent of possible mechanic pairs have ever co-occurred.
3. **Depth costs legibility.** Across mechanics, legibility falls as emergence potential rises (ρ = {{stat:rho_legibility_emergence_potential}}) and as compute cost rises (ρ = {{stat:rho_legibility_compute_cost}}); emergence rises with tuning difficulty (ρ = {{stat:rho_emergence_potential_tuning_difficulty}}).{{assert:rho_legibility_emergence_potential < -0.2}}{{assert:rho_legibility_compute_cost < 0}}{{assert:rho_emergence_potential_tuning_difficulty > 0.2}} Legibility, more than compute, bounds how much coupling a commercial simulation can afford.
4. **AI techniques target what games show and say, not what they are built from.** Exposure concentrates in narrative, control, agent, and physics mechanics.{{assert:top_exposed_family == Narrative & generation}} {{stat:n_mechanics_zero_exposure}} mechanics, carrying {{stat:pct_uses_on_zero_exposure}} percent of all mechanic uses in the catalog, are affected by no technique in the set. Explanation is the rarest effect a technique has on a mechanic ({{stat:n_effect_explains}} of {{stat:n_affects_edges}} edges), although it addresses the binding constraint.{{assert:rarest_effect == Explains}}
5. **What shipped mostly stays at the edge of simulation state.** Neural rendering, asset and motion generation, and language characters for dialogue and companionship have shipped; techniques that would replace rules or physics with learned prediction remain prototypes or research{{assert:n_technique_shipped_mainstream <= 2}}. {{stat:n_technique_breaks_determinism}} techniques in the set break reproducibility outright when used at runtime.
6. **The best near-term opportunities keep the simulation symbolic** and let learned models read, explain, decorate, and propose through validated, logged interfaces. The highest-priority opportunity is {{stat:top_opportunity}}.
