# ADR-0004: Contributor readiness (contract 1.1)

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-10-25
- Contract version: 1.1
- Runs: `2026-10-25-contributor-readiness`, then `2026-10-25-review-round` for the review fixes

## Context

Before inviting outside contributors, many of them AI agents, a review of the repository found five problems that would get worse with more hands, and a set of guardrails the contract lacked.

1. **Record text that contradicted the data.** Two opportunity theses called tech trees "the most common mechanic in the catalog" (third at the time) and production chains "the most-used rule-system" (fifth). Report prose was pinned by assertions; record text was not.
2. **Mechanics that duplicated fields.** `variable-speed` (pause and speed control) and `modding-api` duplicate the game fields `time_model` and `modding`, and the two disagreed: 3 pausable games lacked `variable-speed`, and 29 games with scripting or total-conversion modding lacked `modding-api`, RimWorld among them, although the mechanic's own definition cites it.
3. **Fragile clusters.** k = 16 was picked as the smallest k that separated two city-builder lineages, an outcome-driven choice. Removing any one of 36 of the 174 games broke at least one cluster assertion in §5.4.
4. **Committed projections.** `dist/` was committed and checked byte for byte, so every data PR had to carry thousands of regenerated lines and every pair of concurrent PRs conflicted.
5. **Contributor docs.** README step 4 told catalog runs to scaffold mechanics, which E031 forbids; the E005 hint told contributors to add vocab terms themselves, which AGENTS.md forbids; there was no CONTRIBUTING, code of conduct, contribution licensing, or issue forms.

The guardrails missing for AI contributors: nothing tied a claim to a checkable passage of its source; a model could verify its own model's work under a different run id; scores had no record of who rated them or how consistently; predictions had no resolution field; games had no external ids for other datasets to join on; PR size was unbounded.

## Decision

1. **Record assertions (E032).** Games, mechanics, techniques, and opportunities may carry `assertions` in report-assertion syntax. They are evaluated in every check. Record text that states a dataset finding (a lint over phrases such as "in the catalog", "white-space analysis", "most-used") must carry at least one. New assertion references: `cooccur.<a>.<b>` (games using both mechanics), `cocluster.<a>.<b>` (share of resampled clusterings that group two games), and the derived field `prevalence_rank`. The two false theses were corrected and pinned; four other theses that state dataset findings were pinned.
2. **Field-bound mechanics (E033).** A vocab term may declare `requires_mechanic` or `allows_mechanic`. `time_model: real-time-pausable` requires `variable-speed` and `run-on-demand` allows it; `modding: scripting` and `total-conversion` require `modding-api` and `content` allows it. The `modding` definitions now say what counts as a modding surface (official, or a de facto loader most modders use), and `real-time-pausable` says a menu that freezes the game does not count. Data migrated in this run: `modding-api` added to 25 games; `modding` corrected to `none` for Bitburner, Game of Life, and Hamurabi (no modding surface; the in-game scripting of Bitburner is play, and editing a source listing makes a new program) and to `content` for Smallville, and from `content` to `scripting` for Autonauts (Lua Automods); `variable-speed` added to Mindustry; Spore and Breath of the Wild moved to `real-time` (menu pause only).
3. **Cluster count by a stated stability rule.** `report/analysis.yaml` states the rule: re-cluster 1,000 subsamples of about 80% of the games, compute the mean adjusted Rand index against the full clustering for each k from 2 to 30, and take the smallest k whose mean reaches 0.75. Each game's membership in subsample b is fixed by a hash of (seed, b, game id), so adding or removing a game leaves every other game's memberships unchanged. The 0.75 threshold mirrors the cluster-wise Jaccard convention of Hennig (2007); applying it to mean ARI is this project's choice, not Hennig's. The curve was inspected before the rule was written down; it is flat between roughly k = 18 and 24 and no rule picks a sharp optimum, so a threshold was adopted rather than an argmax. The rule gave k = 24, which is now fixed in config so that a data PR does not reshuffle every cluster; W107 warns only when the rule clearly favours another k (beyond a 0.02 tolerance), and only a maintenance run may re-select it, by the rule. The build also reports cluster-wise stability (mean best-match Jaccard), a co-clustering matrix, and ARI and NMI against subgenre labels with a 999-permutation null. A cluster-membership assertion requires the pair to co-cluster in at least 90% of subsamples (`==`) or at most 10% (`!=`); cluster labels and cluster-table ranks are rejected in assertions; weaker links are stated by comparison (`cocluster.a.b < cocluster.a.c`). §5.4 and the abstract were rewritten accordingly. With the final assertions, removing any one of the 174 games breaks none of them, and neither does adding a duplicate of any of seven tested games (Stardew Valley, Factorio, The Sims 4, RimWorld, Anno 1800, Papers, Please, iRacing); the comparative claims hold across all of those variants too.
4. **dist/ is not committed.** It is gitignored. `build.py --check` builds in memory and compares only the GENERATED blocks (which change only with contract, vocab, or rule edits; the README stats block was removed for that reason). CI proves determinism by building twice under different `PYTHONHASHSEED` values and diffing (`make determinism`). The Pages workflow builds, then publishes `dist/web` as the site with the other projections under `/dist/` plus a zip of all of them.
5. **Contributor docs.** CONTRIBUTING.md (work, review, rules for agents, inbound = outbound licensing with DCO sign-off), CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md (Contributor Covenant 2.1 by reference), issue forms (data error, dispute, verification request, prediction resolution, task), `tasks/` briefs, a corrected README, and an E005 hint that matches AGENTS.md.
6. **Evidence guardrails.** Claims gain `quote` and `locator`, and every claim a PR adds must carry a quote (E039). Sources gain `archived`, a Wayback snapshot that must be of the source's own url (E034). `scripts/check_sources.py` fetches sources and checks quotes and store ids in a separate `sources` workflow, never in the build (I11): a quote missing from a page that holds full text (snapshot, plain url, arXiv HTML) fails; unreachable sources and abstract-only pages are flagged for the reviewer. It blocks merging only once it is made a required check. W105 counts sources without snapshots. `verified` needs a source that is not a wiki, judged by kind and by url host, so relabelling a wiki as documentation does not pass (E034).
7. **Independence (E035).** A run that verifies a record, rates one of its scores, or resolves its prediction must have a different operator from the creating run, and a different model (compared without case or punctuation) when both are agent runs. Because operator, actor, and model are self-declared, the PR check binds the new run's operator to the PR author and forbids changing an existing record's `provenance.created`; the model id still rests on the operator's honesty, which the docs say plainly. Rater agreement counts only independent pairs.
8. **Ratings and calibration (E036, E037).** Records may carry `ratings` (field, value, run, rationale). A field with ratings must include the original score as a rating from the creating run, every other rating comes from a verification run, one operator-and-model gives one rating, and the field stores the lower median. A PR may add ratings only from its own run, plus the creator's original score unchanged (E039). `calibration/mechanics-v1.yaml` freezes 20 mechanics, one per family, chosen greedily to cover the most (scale, anchor) cells; their frozen scores must equal the stored ones, so changing one is a maintainer-reviewed change. Sets are versioned and kept, and each run is judged against the set it names, so retiring a set never invalidates merged runs. A run recording contract 1.1 or later that creates or rates mechanics must record its calibration scores, and the validator computes their within-one agreement, which must reach 0.8. The reference scores are public, so the gate shows a rater reads the anchors as the set does; it cannot prove the rater scored blind or did not rescore until passing. `calibrate.py score` reports only overall agreement to make rescoring less informative. The build reports calibration results and rater agreement.
9. **Prediction resolution (E038, W106).** `falsifiable_signal.resolution` (outcome from the new `prediction_outcome` vocabulary, date, run, evidence, note), set only by an independent verification run, with evidence unless withdrawn; `refuted` only after the resolve-by date, `withdrawn` only before it. W106 lists predictions past their date without one. The predictions table shows each prediction's status.
10. **Store ids.** Games gain `store_ids` (Steam app id, IGDB slug, Wikidata id), format-checked by the schema and resolved against the stores by `check_sources.py`. No ids were backfilled in this run: an unchecked id is worse than none, and `tasks/store-ids.md` asks contributors to add confirmed ones.
11. **PR scope (E039).** `scripts/pr_scope.py` runs on pull requests: at most 10 records and 30 sources (registry `contribution`); exactly one new run file, recording the current contract version, dated within 30 days, with the PR author as operator; every changed record names it, keeps `provenance.created`, and keeps its `reviewed` entries; new ratings come from the PR's run; new claims carry quotes; no deleted records; no edited merged runs; every commit signed off. Maintainer labels `bulk` and `run-typo` lift the limits.
12. **Contract version history.** The registry lists every contract version with its effective date. A run records the version it was written under; E028 rejects a version that did not exist on the run's date or that was already superseded the day before, so a contract bump never forces rewriting run files (I9) and a run cannot backdate itself out of the 1.1 gates.
13. **CODEOWNERS** now covers the report, the analysis parameters, mechanics, opportunities, the calibration set, tasks, and the workflows, in addition to the contract surfaces.

## Consequences

- Prevalence changed for two mechanics: `modding-api` rose to the third most used mechanic, so §5.1's account of the head of the distribution was rewritten and pinned with `prevalence_rank` assertions.
- Clusters changed from 16 to 24. Factory games and settlement builders each hold together and merge in some resamplings; vehicle sims split into flight and driving groups; life sims and colony sims each hold together and often, but less consistently, share a cluster, so §5.4 no longer asserts one family. Tycoon and hospital management games form their own group.
- Every rubric score is still one model's reading. The machinery for a second, calibrated rater exists; no second rating exists yet, and the report says so with a computed count.
- Several checks rest on self-declared fields (operator, actor, model, run date). The PR check ties operator to the PR author and date to the PR's time; the model id and the blindness of calibration depend on contributors' honesty and on review.
- Data PRs now contain records and a run file only. Contributors who want local projections run `make build`.
- Branch protection on `main` (require the `check` and `sources` workflows and code-owner review) must be enabled in the repository settings for CODEOWNERS and the PR checks to block merges.

## Review

An independent review of the first implementation (commit a88eca7) found that the cluster assertions failed when any one of most games was removed or a game was added (subsamples were redrawn by position and pair margins were thin), that several checks could be bypassed (backdated runs, ratings attributed to other runs, rewritten `provenance.created`, cluster labels and unknown right-hand references in assertions, wikis relabelled as documentation, snapshots of other urls), and that some prose overstated the numbers (two "stable" groups below the stability bar, economy mechanics called among the least targeted at ninth of 21 families, an over-strong tagging claim). Items 3, 6 to 9, 11, and 12 above describe the corrected design; §5.1, §5.4, §8.2, §11, and §2.6 were rewritten.
